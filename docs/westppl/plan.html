1 · The idea

A members' club without the building.

Soho House sells two things: a building and a guest list. The building is the expensive part, and it's the part younger members care least about. West Ppl sells the guest list, the calendar and the introductions, and borrows the buildings. Its venues are restaurants, rooftops and houses that are glad to have a full room of the right people on a Tuesday.

Samuel's own words for it are better than any pitch: good people, great places, better stories. The platform's job is to make that repeatable in three cities without Samuel personally texting every guest.

What a member gets

  • A city home: West Ave (Miami), West Village (New York), West Hollywood (LA). Each has its own calendar, waitlist and host.
  • A calendar worth clearing your week for: dinners, rooftops, house parties, morning walks. Most are members-and-guests; some are public, as a way in.
  • One-tap RSVPs, as easy as Partiful, with member pricing and first access.
  • Introductions: a directory and direct messages between approved members, and two introductions each to pass on.
  • Perks from partner venues and brands that want this crowd. This builds on Samuel's own "concierge marketplace" and "West Mag" ideas from the West Ave Group mockup.

Why now

The same shift the Afterglow plan documents applies here. Late-night clubbing is shrinking, Gen Z and millennials drink less and sleep more, and they spend on experiences with people they like. Smaller, earlier, curated nights are the growing part of the market. West Ppl already throws those nights. It just hasn't been paid for the community that keeps coming back.

2 · The secret sauce

Sell the room. Keep the list.

The list is the asset. Today, every time Samuel promotes someone else's event, he posts their link. Each person who buys goes into that promoter's Eventbrite, Dice or Posh account, and from then on the promoter markets to them directly. An affiliate commission is a one-time fee for a permanent piece of the list. Every promotion spends the list down a little.

West Ppl Pass fixes that. When Samuel promotes an event, nobody gets sent off the site. Members buy inside West Ppl at a West Ppl price, often with a perk the public doesn't get. West Ppl sells the ticket, issues the QR code and keeps the buyer. The event gets what it actually wanted, which is the right people in the room. It never gets their contact details.

What the event gets

  • A full room of a crowd that raises the night
  • Payout within 48 hours, minus West Ppl's cut
  • Live sales and check-ins
  • A door app on any phone: first name, party size, valid or not
  • Messages to ticket holders, relayed through West Ppl
  • An after-night report in aggregate: how many came, ages, neighborhoods

What the event never gets

  • Emails
  • Phone numbers
  • Instagram handles
  • A way to reach the crowd again without coming back to West Ppl

Want them again? Book another night on the calendar, or pay for a send to the list through West Ppl. Either way it's a rental, not a sale.

How a partner night works

  1. The dealThe partner gives West Ppl an allocation (say 50 tickets), a West Ppl price below public and a perk: a drink on arrival, reserved seating, early entry.
  2. The promotionIt goes on the West Ppl calendar and in the Thursday email, marked as a partner pick. Members see the deal. The public, if invited, sees the normal price.
  3. The saleThe buyer pays West Ppl (Stripe, Apple Pay). Their QR ticket comes from West Ppl and goes into their Wallet. Their details stay in West Ppl's records.
  4. The doorThe partner scans with the West Ppl door app on any phone. Or, if they run their own scanner, West Ppl sends them the ticket codes only, which are anonymous, so their system can check people in without knowing who they are.
  5. AfterPayout via Stripe Connect, minus West Ppl's 10–20%. The partner gets the attendance report, and Samuel gets one more reason people pay to be members.

Built to work with everyone else's ticketing

Partners don't have to switch systems. West Ppl Pass plugs into whatever they already use. It can hold a reserved block in their ticketing and report sales back as a count. It can hand over anonymous codes for their door scanners. It can put a "Buy on West Ppl" button on their own page that still keeps the buyer with West Ppl. Tickets are standard Apple and Google Wallet passes. The one thing that never moves across is the person.

Does it have legs? Yes.

  • It's a proven shape. Booking.com and Expedia built enormous businesses on it: the hotel gets a guest in the room, the platform keeps the customer. West Ppl is that for nights out, with a far better audience than a search engine.
  • It flips the economics of the list. Each partner night makes membership more valuable (more to do, at member prices) without Samuel producing it, and makes the list bigger and better-known rather than leaking it.
  • It multiplies the calendar. Samuel can host maybe five nights a month. With partners, members can have twenty to choose from.
  • It's the foundation for everything else. Afterglow, brand partnerships and perks all run on the same rails.

The honest risks

  • Some promoters will insist on the data. Offer aggregate reports and paid sends instead, or pass on them. The ones who say yes are the ones who value the crowd.
  • Selling the ticket means owning refunds. If a partner cancels, West Ppl refunds members. So payouts release after the event, and every partner signs a short agreement.
  • Trust is the product. The privacy promise ("organizers never see your details") goes in writing in the member terms, and it has to be kept.

3 · The build

Reuse, build, and what we need.

Samuel asked three questions. Here are the answers.

What we can reuse

  • The brand: the flamingo wordmark, black and blush pink, heavy condensed headlines. It's good. The prototype keeps all of it and adds an italic serif for warmth, a tighter palette and rules for members-only and Afterglow moments.
  • The copy and structure: Home / About / Events / Apply maps directly onto Lobby / Calendar / Members. "Small rooms, good tables, and the people around them" stays.
  • The photography from past events goes into every image slot in the prototype.
  • The West Ave Group mockup (concierge marketplace, curated experiences, West Mag) becomes Phase 3: perks and editorial, both of which make money.
  • The channels: hello@westavegroup.com, @westppl, the domain.

What we build first

A mobile-first web app that installs to a phone's home screen. Not an app-store app yet, so there's no review wait and one codebase.

The simplest way to do messaging

Two kinds, kept separate. Announcements (invitations, event updates, "doors at 8") go out from the host screen as email plus a text to people who opted in. Member-to-member messages are simple one-to-one threads inside the app, only between approved members, with an email nudge when one arrives. No group chats at launch. They're what makes communities feel like work. Add them per city later if members ask.

How it's built

AppNext.js web app, mobile-first, installable. Hosted on Vercel.
Data & loginSupabase: database, login by email link or text code, members-only rules, live messages.
MoneyStripe: memberships, tickets, tables, refunds, Apple Pay. Stripe Connect for partner payouts.
MessagesResend (email) and Twilio (text) for invites, reminders, blasts.
Host screenInside the same app: publish events, review applications, pull lists.

Running cost at launch: roughly $100–300 a month, plus per-text and card fees. We looked at running it on Luma, Partiful or Posh. They're faster on day one, but they own the guest relationship, take a fee on every ticket, and can't do members-only events, per-city waitlists and memberships together. Community tools like Circle or Mighty Networks are weak at events and tickets. West Ppl's whole value is the guest list, so it should own it.

Built in now so the money options are easy later

  • One record per person through their whole life with West Ppl: contact → applicant → member → guest → attendee. Each has a city, tags, how they found us and who introduced them.
  • Every event has ticket types from day one (free, paid, table, comp) even when everything is free, so charging later is a setting, not a rebuild.
  • Check-ins at the door. Real attendance is what sponsors and venues pay for, and what earns members their status.
  • Consent flags for email, text and partner offers, captured at application, so partnerships never put the list at risk.
  • West Ppl is always the seller of record. Every ticket, even a partner's, is issued by West Ppl, so the buyer's record never leaves. Partners see tokens and first names, never contacts.
  • Partners and perks as first-class objects (venue, brand, offer, redemptions) so a sponsor gets a real report.

What we need from Samuel to start

  1. The list as a spreadsheet: name, email, phone, city, how you know them, last event attended (whatever exists).
  2. Access to the current site and domain.
  3. Event photography you're happy to use publicly.
  4. The next 60 days of events: date, venue, capacity, price, public or members.
  5. Your call on membership price, and the first 100 names per city for Founding invites.
  6. Your 5–10 best venue relationships and the deals you have with them (bar minimums, splits).
  7. A Stripe account in the business's name, or the company documents to set one up.
  8. Who besides you reviews applications in New York and LA.

4 · The list

8,000 people who already said yes.

The list is the business. Most event companies spend their marketing budget finding people. Samuel already has 8,000 who've come to something, in the three cities that spend the most on going out. The plan is to turn the people who love West Ppl into members, the people who like it into guests, and keep everyone else warm.

  1. Week 1: clean and sortDedupe, check emails, tag by city and by how recently they came. Three tiers (estimates until we see the real list): A, about 800 who came twice or more this year; B, about 2,400 who came once or engage; C, the rest.
  2. Week 2: the Founding CircleA personal invitation to tier A: 100 founding spots per city at $360 a year, locked for life, with two guest passes a month and first call on Afterglow tables. At a 25–35% yes rate that's 200–280 founding members. At $360 prepaid, that's $72K–$100K in hand within about 60 days.
  3. Weeks 3–4: the Lobby opensTier B gets "The Lobby is open," with a two-week window to apply. Every founding member gets two introductions to pass on. Introductions through friends is the brand, and it's free acquisition.
  4. Month 2 onward: the way inTier C gets public events first (free or cheap RSVP, members free). Come to one, then you can apply. That re-engages people without discounting membership.
  5. Every week: one rhythmA Thursday "This week at West" email per city. Texts only for people who opted in (that's the law). Nothing else. Restraint is part of the brand.

The rule

The list is never sold or shared. Sponsors get presence at events and aggregate insight (for example, "62% of members are 28–40, live in Brickell and Wynwood"), never contacts. The day a member gets a spam email because they joined West Ppl, the list starts dying.

5 · The money

Four ways in. None leaks the list.

Samuel asked where the strongest opportunity is. In order:

  1. Memberships (strongest, do first). Recurring, paid in advance, and it changes his life from night-to-night to month-to-month. It's also what makes the business worth something if it's ever sold.
  2. Tickets through West Ppl Pass: Samuel's nights and partners'. On his own events: member pricing, paid tables, waitlists that fill no-shows, no ticketing-company fees. On partner events: a 10–20% cut of every ticket, and the buyer stays his. This is the line that grows fastest, because it doesn't need Samuel in the room.
  3. Venue and brand partnerships. Venues pay (in bar splits or fees) to fill rooms on slow nights. Brands pay to be in front of a verified, affluent, three-city audience. This becomes real at about 500 paying members and steady check-in data.
  4. Perks members actually value. Smallest money, biggest retention. Priority tables, a hotel rate in the other two cities, early Afterglow access. Commission-based, so it costs nothing to run.

7 · West Ppl × Afterglow

One builds the crowd. One fills the stage.

Afterglow Electronic is the 4pm-to-midnight, 5,000-person electronic music series planned for Miami's October–March season, with Samuel already named as co-founder and COO. On paper it's the bigger business. In practice it carries a risk West Ppl solves, and West Ppl has a ceiling Afterglow lifts.

What West Ppl gives Afterglow

Its ticketing (Pass) and its crowd. Afterglow's biggest risk is selling 5,000 tickets at $150 for a brand nobody has heard of. West Ppl turns that into a presale to people who already trust Samuel's taste.

What Afterglow gives West Ppl

A marquee that makes membership worth more, sponsor packages big enough to matter, and 30,000 attendees a season walking past a "join the Lobby" code.

The recommendation

Run them as one family. West Ppl is the community and the platform, and Afterglow is the flagship that runs on it. Sequence it so each step pays for the next:

  1. December: launch West Ppl in Miami and open the Founding Circle to the list.
  2. January 23: an Afterglow Preview at 1,000 people (the plan's own 1K scenario), sold to West Ppl members first. It proves demand and the format cheaply.
  3. Spring 2027: raise for the Afterglow season with real sell-through numbers and member presale commitments, not projections. That's a much easier conversation for the $700K.
  4. October 2027: Afterglow season one at 5,000, with West Ppl as the presale engine and ticketing platform.

Open question for Eli and Samuel: one company, or two with a revenue share for presale, ticketing and members? It depends on how equity is split today, and it's worth settling before the January preview.
